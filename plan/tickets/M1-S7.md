## Outcome

`plan/decisions/M1-S7.md`: pass, or revisit the API choice. The record names the system calls used for names, paths, close and kill, the trace provider and event that prove the zero, and the shape of the CI job that Milestone 2 turns into a permanent check.

Order matters: the trace on the hosted runner comes first. The lister runs on the owner's PC only after that trace shows zero opens, and then only the listing, never close or kill.

## Out of scope

`ProcessLister` and `ProcessRunner` in `ol-platform-win` (Milestone 2). Close by window message. The Riot adapter.

## Traps

- The whole point: an anti-cheat driver watches for handles opened to its processes. A helper that "just reads the path" through `OpenProcess`, `QueryFullProcessImageName`, the toolhelp module snapshot or any crate that wraps them is the failure this spike exists to rule out. The Electron code already avoids it (spec 8.12): keep its API choice unless the trace disproves it.
- Kill and close act only on children the spike itself started. Never on a process found by name.
- No process tool, in the spike or around it: no `tasklist`, `taskkill`, WMI, `Get-Process`. On the runner the trace is the measuring device; reading it needs none of those either.
- A trace that records nothing proves nothing. The control binary's open must show up before the zero counts.
- The trace needs administrator rights, which the runner has and an agent on the owner's PC must not ask for.
- `sysinfo` and its kind are banned for exactly this reason. The `windows` crate only.
