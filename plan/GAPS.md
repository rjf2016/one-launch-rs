# Known gaps

What is known to be missing, unchecked or unlike the Electron app, and the slice of `LEAN.md` that settles it. Remove a line when it is settled.

## Library (slices 3 and 5)

- The card menu holds "Play as…", "Refresh artwork" and "Choose portrait image…" under the game's name and where its artwork came from. The menu opens at the pointer from the More button or a right-click (on the press of the right button; the Electron app's `contextmenu` comes on its release); cards take no keyboard, so the menu key and Shift+F10 do not open it. It closes on Escape, Tab, a press outside it, a dialog that comes up over it, any turn of the wheel (also over the menu, where the Electron app's scroll listener hears nothing) and a resize. Its first item has the keyboard from the start; Down and Up go round the three, Home and End go to the first and the last, and Enter or Space picks the one that is lit. It is kept 8 px inside the window only when it would cross the edge (GPUI's `snap_to_window_with_margin`); the Electron app keeps it 8 px inside even when it fits.
- On the owner's word the Library is sized as they picked it from a prototype of the page (2026-11-21): the width of the pane alone decides how many cards stand in a row, and the cards share out the row. A card is at least 163 px wide with 6 px between, the page has 24 px at its sides, and the page's scrollbar always keeps a strip of 12 px at the right, whether it shows or not. In the Electron app a card is at least 160 px wide with 16 px between (190 px and 20 px from a window 1536 px wide), the page has 32 px at its sides, and the scrollbar takes its 12 px only while the page scrolls, so a drag of the window's edge takes columns away and gives them back. A row here holds more cards than there: six in the window as it first opens, where the Electron app shows five. The Recent strip's scrollbar takes no room: it lies over the gap under the strip.
- The sidebar starts folded to its icons at every window width, on the owner's word (2026-11-21), and its button and Ctrl+B open and fold it. The Electron app opens it from a window 1100 px wide and keeps the user's choice in its page storage (`sidebar`). Here a toggle stores the choice in the row `sidebar` of the settings table (`expanded` or `collapsed`), as `triage.toml` X-01 decides, and a start reads it; no row, or any other value, is folded. The choice made in the Electron app is not taken over: it resets once. The stored choice is asked of the database while the window opens, before the Library's games, so a sidebar stored open can show folded in the frames drawn before the answer, which have no games in them yet; this was not seen in a capture. The fold is not animated: the Electron app widens the sidebar over 200 ms and fades the labels over 150 ms, and an animated width would have the grid laid out afresh at every frame of it.
- The Recent strip goes back to its start when the list is shown again (a search, a filter, a reload), and Shift with the wheel does nothing over the grid.
- The Library reads its games and the Recent strip again each time it is shown, so an account renamed or deleted on the Accounts screen shows at once on the cards ("removed account" for a deleted one). Its posters stay decoded when the list is the same.
- "No launchers found" comes from the last scan alone. The Library does not ask for the launchers' status (`Backend::launcher_statuses`), so a start whose scan fails says "No games found".
- The scan a start runs is asked for by the window, after the Library was first read. The Electron app's main process runs it; the result on screen is the same.
- Not seen in a capture, because each lasts a few milliseconds on fake launchers: "Scanning your launchers…" with its disabled button, "Scanning…" on Rescan, and the turning Play mark of a one-click Play.
- A database that cannot be opened (damaged, from a newer version, unreadable) ends the start before the window is made, with the Electron app's box (see Start, one copy and the window's place). A read that fails later says "Could not load the library: …" in place of the grid, as there.
- A read of the Library that fails after it has shown its games, after a scan or a launch, leaves the games on screen and says "Could not load the library: …" beside Rescan for six seconds, as a failed scan is told. The Electron app puts that sentence in place of the grid until a read succeeds. On purpose: the games on screen are the last ones read, and a passing fault no longer empties the page.
- The Filter and Sort menus take the mouse and Escape only: no arrow keys, Home, End or Enter, and they stay open while the window is resized.
- Names sort by their lower-cased characters. The Electron app sorts them by the rules of the system language; `triage.toml` X-02 asks for the order of the Windows regional settings, through a native call.
- A poster appears at once, without the 200 ms fade, and the Update badge and the More button have no blur behind them.
- A poster is decoded into a box of 240 by 360 px times the display scale, whatever the card's size.
- The search field has no accessible name or role.
- A click on the empty track of the page's scrollbar does not page, and its thumb shows only while the pointer is below the header.
- The whole card takes the click, its caption included. In the Electron app only the poster does; a click on the name or the line under it does nothing there (`src/renderer/src/components/GameCard.tsx:60-62`).

- The dialogs take the mouse, Enter and Escape. Enter presses the button drawn with the ring. There is no Tab order, no focus trap, and the keyboard does not go back to what opened the dialog.
- A tooltip that was waiting to show when a click opened a dialog over its item comes up over the dialog and stays until the pointer moves: GPUI checks the hover of a waiting tooltip by the item's bounds alone (`gpui-pre` `elements/div.rs`, `handle_tooltip_mouse_move`). Seen with "Switch to this account".

- No line for a screen reader says what the job is doing.
- Not seen in a capture, because each lasts a few milliseconds on fake launchers: "Checking Steam…" and "Stopping…".

## Data (slices 2 and 6)

- Neither build has read a vault key the other wrapped. The Rust vault is checked against the recorded vectors in `oracle/takeover/vault/` and against a key of its own under real DPAPI. The owner's `--doctor` run on a copy of real data settles reading; `oracle/takeover/vault/make-local-fixture.mjs` would settle both directions.
- A vault key that does not unwrap is replaced only by a reset the user asks for (`Backend::reset_vault`, `OsCryptVault::replace_lost_key`); until then the vault reads as "Key lost", refuses to seal (`VAULT_UNAVAILABLE`) and to open (`VAULT_UNREADABLE`), and the accounts stay readable. The Electron app's Chromium makes a new key at the start that finds one it cannot unwrap. A start with `--real-launchers` comes up that way too (`Backend::start_on_this_pc`), with the launchers sealing through the backend's one vault, so a reset's new key is the key of every seal after it, the switch's included; a Windows that wraps nothing just now, or cannot unwrap the new key it wrapped, leaves it unavailable with nothing written, to be tried again at the next look, and only a key file in another shape still stops that start. Not run with a real key of another user: the tests stand a protector of their own in for DPAPI.
- Windows names no error of `CryptUnprotectData` that says a key belongs to another user or PC: Microsoft's page for the call lists none, and says that data that was tampered with can fail with `ERROR_INVALID_DATA` or `ERROR_INVALID_PARAMETER` and that callers should not rely on a specific code. So a key counts as lost only while Windows wraps and unwraps a value of the app's own, and the key fails a second try after that; when that fails too, the vault reads as unavailable and no reset is offered or done. A key that did not open is tried again at every read of the vault's state (the Encryption panel, the banner) and by the reset itself, which then removes nothing when the key opens. What cannot be told apart: a key that would unwrap later for a reason only Windows knows (a domain user's PC away from the network after a password change). The confirmation of the reset says that what it deletes is gone even if Windows could read it later.
- A reset refuses while the secrets can be read (`BAD_REQUEST`) and while Windows unwraps nothing (`VAULT_UNAVAILABLE`). It replaces a lost key only when the key in `Local State` is still the one that did not unwrap. Then it removes, row by row, each password, saved sign-in and named secret the vault's key cannot open, and keeps each one it opens; the note says how many it kept. Unlike the Electron app, which clears all of them: after a start that made a new key because `Local State` was gone, what was stored since is sealed with the new key and stays. A clear that fails after the key was replaced leaves the old secrets beside the new key, which reads as "Key lost" again, and a second reset finishes. With no probe stored, the reset first stores one that no key opens, so that holds then too, after a start as well; it counts among the other secrets removed. A key that opens only on the reset's own try beside a probe it does not open is cleared like a lost one, not answered as readable. A probe that cannot be stored after the clear does not fail the reset. A clear that fails, and a probe that cannot be stored, have no test of the whole reset.
- A switch that needs the vault while it has no key (Key lost or unavailable) is refused before anything is closed: the preflight shows it as blocked and a start is refused (`VAULT_UNAVAILABLE`), pointing to Settings › Advanced. Epic, Battle.net and Riot always need it (their snapshots and saved sign-ins are sealed); Steam only to hand over a stored password with automatic sign-in on. A switch whose saved sign-in (Steam: stored password) the vault's key does not open, as after a start that made a new key, is refused the same way (`VAULT_UNREADABLE`), telling the user to store it again. A launch straight to the game, with nothing switched, is not refused. The Electron app closes the launcher and stops at the first seal or open.
- A start stores the vault probe when it is absent and the vault can seal one, as the Electron app's does. The vault opens at start with `--real-launchers`, with fake launchers (the stand-in), and otherwise with the data folder's own key under DPAPI (`Backend::start_with_vault_of_data_folder`), which a first run makes; a backend started with `Backend::start`, which the app no longer does, has none and refuses to store a password (`VAULT_UNAVAILABLE`).
- The check that refuses the real data folder does not look at links inside an allowed folder: `onelaunch.db` as a hard link to the real database, or `chromium` as a junction into the real folder. A mapped network drive and a `subst` drive were not tried.
- That check and its plain-path rule go away in the installed build (slice 6), which runs on the real folder.
- A release build has no console, so `--doctor` there needs its output redirected to a file.
- `Secret::expose()` returns the text, and the fields of `Account`, `Game` and `Settings` are public. Their `Debug` output is redacted, but a caller can still format a field. Look at every such call in the security review.
- `oracle/fixtures/README.md` reads as 2 accounts with passwords; the fixture database holds 3. The tests assert 3. The README is the owner's to correct.

## Log (slices 3 to 5)

What is there: `<data folder>\logs\onelaunch.log`, rotated at 1 MiB into `onelaunch.1.log` to `onelaunch.5.log`, with the Electron app's line format, levels and redaction (`ol_services::log`, the account names in `ol_model::redact`). Every oracle vector of `redactString`, `redactValue` and `formatLine` that a Rust value can carry passes but `names-dotless-i` (see below); the others are left out by name in `crates/ol-services/tests/log_vectors.rs`. The level follows the logLevel setting from the database's first read and at once on a change, Settings › Advanced › Log level included; `[main] starting` and `database migrated` come before that read and are written at INFO whatever is stored, as in the Electron app. `ol-app` opens the log of the data folder for every run but `--doctor` (which writes nothing to the folder), after the refusals of the real data folder, registers the Windows user's profile folder and name for masking before the first line, hands the log to the backend in every start, and closes it after the backend has shut down, so `exit` is the last line. A capture or a probe logs into its scratch folder like any run.

- The window's own lines, in `ol_services::window::WindowLog` (`Backend::window_log`), scope `main`: as in the Electron app, `window hidden to the tray` (INFO, the close button), `tray icon unavailable; closing the window quits` (WARN, the failure's code, `TRAY_WINDOW` or `TRAY_REFUSED`, and Windows' HRESULT, not Windows' sentence) and `stopping the running switch` (WARN, `{"reason":"quit confirmed during a switch"}`, after "Quit anyway" while a switch runs). Beyond the Electron app: `window hidden to the tray after a launch` (INFO, the job's id), where the Electron app writes `window hidden to the tray after an instant launch` for its narrower rule (see Tray); and, of lines it does not write at all, `window kept after a launch` (INFO, the job's id and `direct`, `handOff`, `tray`, `closeToTray`) when a game's Play ended well with "Hide One Launch after Play" on and the window shown, and another condition kept the window, `tray icon added` and `tray icon removed` (INFO, at start, at a change of the setting and at exit), `window shown from the tray` (INFO, the tray's Open or a click on the icon), `quit prompt shown during a switch` (INFO, the job's id) and `quit prompt answered` (INFO, `keep` or `quit`), `window close requested` (DEBUG, `close`, `hide` or `ask`), and `fatal during startup` (ERROR, no fields) when the window cannot be opened. The console no longer prints these, nor `database copied`.
- `launch command sent` (INFO, scope `main:launch`) is written when a launch step ends well, with the job's id, `seen` (the launcher was seen starting the game), `handOff` (a hand-over was left) and `detail`, the step's `Sent …` text, whose program path is masked as `[PATH]` like any other. A launch that fails writes `launch job failed` or `launch job refused`, as before. The Electron app writes a line of that name only in its Battle.net adapter (`codename`, `form`, `started`, `stopped`) and its Riot adapter (`product`, `clientRunning`, `started`, `pageShown`, `declined`, `outOfDate`, `loginPage`, `accepted`, `stopped`, `play`), under the adapter's scope and before it has decided how the launch ended, and `launching game` before every launch in all four adapters. This app writes neither of those, and one line for every launcher instead, so the log says what the app knew when a launch ended.
- No absolute path of the user's is written. Before the redaction, every line's message and every text of its fields go through `ol_services::log::paths`: the data folder, in any case and with either slash, is written `<data folder>`, and any other path from a drive letter or a share is written `[PATH]`, from its start up to a quote, a line's end or a sign that ends a sentence, so a path with spaces is masked whole and a few words after it may go too. Where the Electron app writes the path of the data folder (`starting`'s `dataDir`) this app writes what kind it is, `chosen` in every run of this build, which refuses the default place until slice 6; `database copied` gives its file inside the data folder (`backups\db\…`) and an unreadable snapshot its folder there (`backups\steam\…`). The Electron app writes the full paths, its errors' paths too.
- A panic writes `uncaught exception` at ERROR with its place (`file:line:column`, a dependency's file from its crate's folder, not from the profile) and its thread's name, and waits up to 1 s for the line to reach the disk before the panic goes on (`ol_services::log::log_panics`). It never writes the message: the Electron app writes the error's message and stack, but a Rust panic's message can quote the text it was slicing (up to 256 bytes, a typed password among them) or the `Debug` of a dependency's error, and no rule can tell a shapeless password or a name not yet registered in it. The line takes neither the names' lock nor the redaction, so a panic on any thread is written with its place. A build without debug assertions does not hand the panic on to Rust's own report, which would put the message and a backtrace wherever a tester sent the output; a debug build does. The crash box follows the line (see Start, one copy and the window's place). `unhandled rejection` is not built: Rust has no counterpart for it. The 1 s cap was not driven: it needs a disk that stops answering.
- A panic on the log's own writer thread ends the log for the rest of the run, and nothing says so; the next start writes again.
- ureq and ureq-proto write each request to the `log` crate: ureq at debug level, with only the headers it counts as not sensitive (`DebugHeaders` in `ureq-3.5.2/src/util.rs`, which leaves `authorization` out), and ureq-proto at trace level, every byte of the request's head as hex, `authorization` with it (`Writer`'s drop in `ureq-proto-0.7.1/src/util.rs`, from `client/sendreq.rs`). The app installs no logger, and nothing it runs does (in the pinned sources, `log::set_logger` is called by no crate of the workspace; of the others only by cbindgen while it builds, by the command-line tools of resvg and usvg, and by GPUI for the web), so `log`'s level stays off and those lines are never made. None may be installed without masking `authorization` first: it carries the SteamGridDB key and the Riot Client's password of the run.
- Not logged, for want of a place in the Rust code: the startup timings (the console has the first frame's), the window's lines that have no counterpart (blocked navigation, blocked `window.open`, renderer gone, renderer error), `switch still running at quit` (this app waits for the switch's own timeouts instead of 10 s), the Riot Client's own steps (launch request, Play pressed) as lines of their own (`launch command sent` gives what came of them in its detail), the adapters' scan warnings and debug lines (an unreadable manifest, a candidate folder without the launcher, a process list that could not be read), the kill after a launcher did not close in time, the launcher's own `… session stored` with its fingerprint (a Save sign-in writes `session captured` and `session captured by user` as the Electron app does, but not that line) or an account id linked, updates, `secret stored` and `secret deleted` of the self-test, and `adapter call refused` and `adapter call failed` beyond Play and a restore. A health check's detail is not logged at all. `autostart updated` is there and never written: Start with Windows refuses in this build.
- A line's fields are a JSON value: an error is its code and its message as two fields, where the Electron app writes an object with the error's name, message and stack. A failure inside a switch before anything was written is `switch failed` at ERROR, with no code: the pipeline keeps a failure's text and not its code, where the Electron app writes `switch refused` at WARN for a refusal. `switch rolled back from backup` gives the snapshot's id alone, without the counts of files and registry values.
- Beyond the Electron app, each for a value its rules let through: the patterns see the text before the names are masked, so a name inside an email or a token no longer splits it; a name is masked also as it was written where its lower case folds to something else (İ, ẞ, the Kelvin, Ohm and Angstrom signs, ϴ; the vector `names-dotless-i` expects "İstanbul" left as it is, and is left out); the Windows user's name and the last part of the profile folder are masked wherever they stand whole, also on a share or a redirected folder; a launcher's id for an account (6 characters or more) is masked like a name, so a SteamID64 or a Riot id shows as `[ACCOUNT]` where the Electron app writes `[STEAMID]` or `[UUID…xxxx]`; the end of a launcher's id that a message gives after an ellipsis (Epic's and Battle.net's "signed in as … (account …1a2b3c)") is masked; a key and its value are found also when the quotes around them are escaped (`password\":\"…`); fields that a rule leaves no longer reading as JSON are written as one JSON string; a cut through a surrogate pair writes U+FFFD, which is what the Electron app's file holds there; and in development the stored level is followed too, where the Electron app always logs at debug.
- Beyond the Electron app, for the file: a rotation moves the live file aside before it touches the older ones, so a live file another program holds is not rotated, nothing older is lost and the lines are still appended; and a `logs` folder, a log file or a rotated file that is a link or a junction is not written through. A hard link is not told apart.
- Left as the Electron app has them, and the vectors require: a label of the stop list (`main`, `alt`, `steam`, `user`, …) and a name of 2 characters are never masked, so an account named "Main" is written as it is; a name with a Kelvin sign or a long s in the text, where the name was registered without one, is a different name; a token without a digit and without a key in front of it is not masked; the tail of One Launch's own ids stays (`[UUID…abcd]`) in a message; in a line's fields the second pass over the JSON masks that tail too, as the end of a launcher's id after an ellipsis, so a job or account id there reads `[UUID…[REDACTED]]`.
- A line is made, and redacted, on the thread that logs it, and a thread of its own appends it. Past 4096 lines waiting, a line is dropped and the next batch says how many; this was not driven.
- Never written by a test: `the accounts could not be read`, `the vault probe could not be stored`, `rescan after a launcher folder change failed`, `could not move the unreadable switch journal aside`, `could not record the account's last use` and `could not record launch history` (each needs the database or the disk to fail at one exact call), `adapter call failed`, `autostart updated`.
- Not run: a log on a real data folder, and the replay with a log. The WARN and ERROR lines of the `oracle/l2` recordings are the Electron app's for each scenario; comparing them needs the replay's aliases for job and account ids and the differences above settled.

## Steam (slice 3)

What is there: on fake launchers the backend finds Steam, scans its games into the Library, lists and imports the accounts it remembers, plans and runs a switch (snapshot, journal, the writes, rollback, restore, recovery of an interrupted switch) and starts a game. Proven against the oracle: the 21 files of `oracle/l1/steam` and the two path files of `oracle/l1/core` (1,468 of 1,482 cases; the 14 others hold half a surrogate pair, a fraction or a number where the Rust type takes only text or a whole number); eight scripts of `oracle/l2` that turn on Steam alone, and the Steam jobs of `import` and `handover`; five of the Steam adapter's goldens in `oracle/tests`.

- The real ports are in `ol-platform-win` (see Real ports) and reach the app only through `--real-launchers`, in the build without the feature `fake`. Without it `Backend::start` has no launchers: every launcher command answers `NOT_IMPLEMENTED` and a scan finds nothing. No real switch has been run yet; the first is the owner's.
- The app reaches the fakes only when built with the feature `fake` and started with `--fake-launchers=<folder>`. The fake machine's file port reaches that folder and `backups\` of the data folder, and nothing else. The folder is held to the plain-path rule of the data folder, and a scenario is laid down only in an empty or a new folder. Refused are a folder that holds anything but a scenario's own files and folders, and one whose `steam\steam.exe`, `EpicGamesLauncher.exe`, `Battle.net.exe`, `Battle.net Launcher.exe` or `riot\RiotClientServices.exe` is not an empty file. A folder that passes both is taken at its word.
- A fake machine in the app keeps real time (`FakeClock::following_the_system`), so the age of a remembered session is counted from today: from 2026-11-10 on, `user_1` of the fixtures is past 60 days and every plan for it carries the warning about an expired session, which the Electron app's shots of 2026-11-06 do not show.
- The fake machine's registry is built afresh from the scenario at every start of the app, as the Electron app's is: what a switch wrote there, Steam's `AutoLoginUser` and Battle.net's token under `UnifiedAuth`, is gone after a restart, and a Battle.net token the scripted client issued in an earlier run is unknown to it. The files under the fake launchers' folder stay.
- The replay compares a recorded run whole (`ol_replay::check`): every platform event, the job events, and of the final state the accounts, the four launchers' games and saved sign-ins (each by the digest of what the vault sealed), the launch history, the settings, the journal, the backups (a Battle.net snapshot's tokens by the digest of what was sealed), `loginusers.vdf`, `GameUserSettings.ini`, `Battle.net.config`, `RiotGamesPrivateSettings.yaml` and every registry value. Where a recording gives the digest of a projection, because the scripted Riot Client stamps the time into the file it writes, the replay's content is projected the same way: the two token times masked. The artwork rows are compared too, with the one rule of Artwork below for a Riot game. Not compared: the vault probe, because the replay stores none; the WARN and ERROR lines each recording keeps of `onelaunch.log`, because the replay hands its backend no log yet (see Log); and the copies to the clipboard, because the replay passes `accounts:copy` over and hands the backend no clipboard.
- All 22 scripts of `oracle/l2` are replayed whole. `first-run` is replayed with its two reads of `launchers:status` passed over: the replay does not ask for the launchers' status, and a read leaves nothing behind.
- A `crash` step of the replay does not end a process: it leaves the switch's thread standing at the wait it is in, for good, and starts the app again beside it. What is on disk is what a crash would leave; the first database connection stays open until the test ends.

What is there: on fake launchers the backend finds the Riot Client (the folder chosen in Settings, the client's Uninstall entry, `RiotClientInstalls.json`, the command of the `riotclient` link, the default folder), scans its games into the Library (the metadata folders under `%ProgramData%\Riot Games`, the last play from `RiotClientSettings.yaml`, an update the newest run's log reports), names the one account whose sign-in `RiotGamesPrivateSettings.yaml` holds, says what is saved of an account's sign-in (`Backend::session`), plans and runs a switch (the refusal while a Riot game runs, the window close of the client and then of the window processes it leaves behind, the outgoing sign-in saved, the encrypted snapshot, the write of the file, the start with the game's launch arguments, the sign-in read from the client's log and the account read from the file, the hand-over, the new sign-in saved, a wrong account, cancel, rollback, restore, recovery) and hands a game to the client (the shortcut's two arguments, the watch of the log and of the image names, the press of Play through a port, the hand-over to the user when Play is not pressed or does not take, the wait while the client says the product is not up to date). - Riot's rules (`docs/adapters/riot.md` in the Electron repo) are held by how the code is cut where it can be. The adapter is handed the files and the registry to read only. All it can do to a process goes through `ClientProcesses` (`ol-adapters/src/riot/processes.rs`): the image names that run, a start, and a window close or an end of the client's own two images, the end never with what the image started; there is no way through it to a path or to another image. The Riot switch (`ol-services/src/switch/riot.rs`) reaches no process and no registry port itself. A Riot game is known by `ProcessLister::running_names`, a listing that opens no process, and a listing that fails is an error to the switch, the restore and the launch. The one function that closes the client, `shutdown`, looks for a Riot game directly before every close and every end, the later rounds included, and closes or ends nothing beside one. The real port lists that way, from the kernel's process table, with no `tasklist`, `taskkill`, WMI or `Get-Process`; `ProcessLister::list_processes`, which the Steam, Epic and Battle.net adapters ask for the path of a game of theirs, gives a Riot game, Vanguard and what they started by name alone (see Real ports).

The switch, the rollback and the restore each take that look after the snapshot and the store, directly before they write. Nothing is deleted, and nothing in the registry is written.
- As in the Electron app, and so not a promise: a store that fails does not stop the write that follows it. In a switch the step then reads "Not saved" and the snapshot still holds the file; before a rollback there is no other copy, and before a rollback or a restore nothing says so, because nothing logs yet. The last look is one process listing and then one read of the file, which has to be the file the store read, by the SHA-256 of its text: a file that changed since, because the client ran and exited in between, or that is gone or cannot be read (a file that is not UTF-8 text among them, so a restore over one is refused too), refuses the write with nothing written. What remains is the time from that listing to the write: a Riot game or the client that starts in it is not seen, unless the client has changed the file by the time of the read. A read of the file that fails while the client runs, after a sign-in by hand, fails the switch and puts the file back. A line inside the `riot-client` block that is indented with a tab ends the block for the reader. A comment no deeper than `riot-client:` with more of the block below it is refused as a shape the reader does not know, so nothing is saved from such a file and no sign-out is written over it: the Electron app cuts the block at the comment and writes the rest of it back under `riot-client: null`, which the client cannot read. The client writes neither. Each is the owner's to decide once the real ports show what a real client does.
- A saved sign-in rests in two places: the column `blob_enc` of `launcher_sessions`, sealed by the vault, and a snapshot's copy of the file, sealed the same way as `Data/RiotGamesPrivateSettings.yaml.enc`. In memory the file's text is wiped after use wherever the Riot code holds it (`RiotSessionBlob`, `Secret`, the JSON that is sealed, which is written into a buffer that never grows); the plan keeps only a fingerprint. What a snapshot reads of a file before it seals it, and what a restore takes out of the vault, is wiped too, for every launcher. Not wiped: what the file port holds inside itself while it reads and writes, and the client's log, which is read as text, up to 8 MB at a time, and on a real client names accounts. The test `a_sign_in_is_in_the_clients_own_file_and_sealed_in_the_data_folder_and_nowhere_else` of `ol-api` holds a switch, its plan, its job, its errors, the fake machine's log and every file of the data folder to that, for the tokens, the cookies and the Riot ID.
- The launch request to the client's own local server goes through the port `RiotLocalLaunch` of `ol-platform-api`. On fake launchers `FakeRiotLaunch` hands it to the scripted client as a press of Play and reads no lockfile. With `--real-launchers` the channel is `ol_net::RiotLoopback` (`crates/ol-net/src/riot.rs`), which `real_launchers()` of `ol-app` hands to the Riot machine on the real file port, and which no other source outside `ol-net` names (`the_riot_channel_is_handed_out_by_real_launchers_alone`). A machine without a channel (`RiotMachine`'s default) leaves Play to the user.
  - The call: a stopped job reads and sends nothing; the product and the patchline are checked again (`a` to `z`, digits, `_`) before anything is read; `Config\lockfile` is read through the file port, at most 4 KiB, and taken through `parse_lockfile`, its one caller; then one `POST https://127.0.0.1:<port>/product-launcher/v1/products/<product>/patchlines/<patchline>` goes out with no body and these header lines and no others: `host: 127.0.0.1:<port>`, `authorization: Basic …` (`riot:` and the lockfile's password), `accept: application/json`, `content-length: 0`, `connection: close`. Those are what Node sends for the Electron app's request (seen through `node:http` on Node 24 with the same options, to a listener on 127.0.0.1). Only the status is kept and the body is not read: 2xx is taken, any other status is told by its number, a failure in fixed words ("timed out", "refused", "TLS failed", "error").
  - Its limits: its agent is its own, apart from the artwork client's, which still checks certificates. The host is a constant; the agent's resolver answers 127.0.0.1 and the port the address names for an https address on 127.0.0.1 and nothing else, and looks no name up. No proxy is used, also when the environment names one; no redirect is followed (a 3xx is told as its status after one connection); https only; no connection is kept for a later call (`connection: close`, and no idle connection in ureq's pool); 10 s for the whole request. The server's certificate is not checked, as the Electron app does not check it, which is acceptable only because that agent reaches nothing but 127.0.0.1; clippy refuses `disable_verification` anywhere else in the workspace. The channel holds a file reader and an HTTP agent and no process port, and logs nothing.
  - Held by how the code is cut and by a test of `ol-net` seen failing once when the code was broken: the constant host, the resolver (also through the channel's own agent: a request for `localhost` or another host is refused with no connection made), no proxy, no redirect, the 10 s, the certificate left unchecked in this agent alone (and clippy's refusal elsewhere), `connection: close`, the stop, the product check, the 4 KiB read, each fixed word, and a made-up password kept out of the errors of failed calls. Held by the test of the agent's settings only: https only (the resolver refuses http as well) and the empty idle pool; a second call over a kept connection fails a test only when both the pool's limit and `connection: close` are taken out.
  - Wiped when the call returns: the bytes read, their text, the password, the credentials and the header's text, which is encoded in place into a buffer sized up front. Not wiped: the header value ureq holds (marked sensitive, which only hides it from `Debug`), ureq's and rustls's buffers, and what the file port's read leaves behind. The port's error can carry only fixed words and a status: that is the type (`Copy`, a `&'static str` and a `u16`), and the tests check the errors real calls return, in every failure, and every byte written, for the made-up password.
  - No request has been sent to a real Riot Client by anyone, and no real lockfile has been read. The tests talk to a transport of their own and to listeners of their own on 127.0.0.1, each connecting only to a port whose listener the test holds open until the connection is taken, so no other program on the PC can answer; none of them is a TLS server (see "Only the owner can check"). - An account's page saves the client's current sign-in (`sessions:capture`) and forgets one (`sessions:forget`), as for Epic. A save refuses while the client runs, as in the Electron app, and also beside a Riot game and when the process list cannot be read, from one listing by image names before the file is read and one after it. - Of the 84 Riot adapter goldens of `oracle/tests/goldens`, 77 are not checked, and of the seven that are, only the plans and preflights: the real switches, restores and launches need the set-up of each Electron test rebuilt.

## Real ports (slices 3 and 4)

What is there: `ol-platform-win` has a real registry, process, file, shell and clock port, and `machine::platform()` puts them together. The app attaches them only with `--real-launchers`, and opens the data folder's vault for them (`ol_api::launchers::on_this_pc`). The registry and file ports pass the contract suite the fakes pass (`ol_platform_api::contract`); the process port is tested on copies of `ol-test-child` and, for Riot's games and Vanguard, on a made-up process table behind its `System` seam.

- Not run on a real launcher by anyone: a scan, a close, an end, a start, a link, a write. What the real launchers do with them is the owner's first switch to show.
- Riot's launch request has a real channel, `ol_net::RiotLoopback` over the real file port, and only a start with `--real-launchers` hands it to the Riot machine (see Riot). It has never sent a request to a real Riot Client.
- The process table is read with `NtQuerySystemInformation` (`SystemProcessInformation` for the names, ids, parents and start times, `SystemProcessIdInformation` for a file), as the Electron app's own scripts read it; the row layout is checked on every read against this process's own name and start time, and a table that does not agree is an error.
- A kill chooses its targets from the table, reads the table again just before it opens them and drops any whose row is gone, changed or now shielded; a path is asked only of a process the table shows unchanged just before, and dropped when it has changed just after. What is left: a process that ends between that last read and the call, and whose id Windows hands at once to a new process, has that new process opened (by a kill) or its path asked (by a listing). A kill then ends nothing, as the start time of what it opened is not the one chosen, and a listing drops the path. The same holds for a window handle between the listing of the windows and the close. A port that acts by id or by handle cannot close that window.
- A Riot game or Vanguard shields what it started through the parent ids in the table, and a process whose parent has exited is linked to nothing there. So the port remembers, by id and start time, each process it saw shielded, and shields it and whatever it starts for as long as it runs; every listing, close and end reads the table. That holds only for a link the port read while it stood: a helper whose go-between exited before any read of this run is a stranger to the port. Its path is asked by `list_processes` (from the kernel's table of ids and images: it is not opened), and a close or an end that names its own image reaches it; the adapters name only a launcher's own images. No tree end reaches it, as an id taken again belongs to a younger process, which is no parent (a clock set back aside). What is remembered can also be too much: a process shielded because its parent's id was taken by a Riot game stays shielded until it exits, so an end leaves it and the launcher's shutdown then says it is still running.
- A put-back that itself fails leaves the file named in the error with its new content and its old content beside it, as `<file>.onelaunch-<pid>-<count>-<clock>.old`. The old content is kept by a hard link; where the disk cannot make one, by a copy, and a target put back from a copy is a new file with the old bytes, without the old one's attributes or permissions.
- The protected images are `ol_platform_api::protected::PROTECTED_IMAGES`. A Riot game that ships under a new image name is not protected until it is added there; a test of `ol-adapters` holds the list to every game image of `ol-formats`' product table.
- A program started by its 8.3 path runs under a short name such as `VALORA~1.EXE`. A name with `~` and a digit whose first two characters, spaces left out, are those of a protected image is protected too; any other program whose name starts so and runs under a short name is shielded as well, which costs nothing, and none of the launchers' own images does. What counts as a Riot game running is narrower, because a stranger's short name there would refuse every Riot switch: the game image's first six characters, `~` and a number, or its first two, four hex digits, `~` and a number (Vanguard's never count). Whether Windows reports a short name for such a process at all is not verified.

## Differences from the Electron app, on purpose

None of those about stored values and launcher files is visible on data the app wrote itself. The last four are about the window and the fake launchers.

- A stored value shorter than 31 bytes, the empty one included, is `VAULT_UNREADABLE`. So is a decrypted value that is not UTF-8.
- Integer settings parse strictly: `35px` is not 35.
- A non-text value in a text column fails the read and names the column.
- Artwork comes back as the stored relative path, not as an `onelaunch-art://` URL. The Library gets the file's path, joined only when the stored one has the shape `<game id>/<kind>.<ext>`.
- `PRAGMA temp_store = MEMORY` on every open, so rows cannot spill into `%TEMP%`.

## Start, one copy and the window's place (slice 5)

- One running copy per data folder (`ol_platform_win::instance`): a named mutex `Global\OneLaunch.Instance.<hash>`, which a copy the same user runs in another Windows session meets too, the hash of the folder as the file system resolves it (`data_folder::identity`: a junction, a link, a short name, a `subst` drive, another case and a trailing slash name the same folder; the case is Windows' own, one character for one, so `straße` and `strasse` are two folders with two locks), taken before the log, the database and the vault are opened, as the Electron app's lock is. On the window's thread nothing can run on: unwinding there would end the process through GPUI's window procedure. - The window's place is the row `window.bounds`, in the Electron app's form and key order (`{"x":…,"y":…,"width":…,"height":…,"maximized":…}`), stored by the app alone (`Backend::remember_window_bounds`; `set_setting` refuses the key): 500 ms after the window was last moved or resized, and when it is closed, hidden to the tray or quit. Otherwise the window opens 1280 by 800 in the middle of the primary display's work area. Maximised counts whatever the place.
