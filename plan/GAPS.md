# Known gaps

What is known to be missing, unchecked or unlike the Electron app, and the slice of `LEAN.md` that settles it. Remove a line when it is settled.

## Library (slices 3 and 5)

- The More button of a card and a right-click on it do nothing: there is no card menu ("Play as…", "Refresh artwork", "Choose portrait image…").
- On the owner's word the Library is sized as they picked it from a prototype of the page (2026-11-21): the width of the pane alone decides how many cards stand in a row, and the cards share out the row. A card is at least 163 px wide with 6 px between, the page has 24 px at its sides, and the page's scrollbar always keeps a strip of 12 px at the right, whether it shows or not. In the Electron app a card is at least 160 px wide with 16 px between (190 px and 20 px from a window 1536 px wide), the page has 32 px at its sides, and the scrollbar takes its 12 px only while the page scrolls, so a drag of the window's edge takes columns away and gives them back. A row here holds more cards than there: six in the window as it first opens, where the Electron app shows five. The Recent strip's scrollbar takes no room: it lies over the gap under the strip.
- The sidebar starts folded to its icons at every window width, on the owner's word (2026-11-21), and its button and Ctrl+B open and fold it. The Electron app opens it from a window 1100 px wide and keeps the user's choice in its page storage (`sidebar`). Here a toggle stores the choice in the row `sidebar` of the settings table (`expanded` or `collapsed`), as `triage.toml` X-01 decides, and a start reads it; no row, or any other value, is folded. The choice made in the Electron app is not taken over: it resets once. The stored choice is asked of the database while the window opens, before the Library's games, so a sidebar stored open can show folded in the frames drawn before the answer, which have no games in them yet; this was not seen in a capture. The fold is not animated: the Electron app widens the sidebar over 200 ms and fades the labels over 150 ms, and an animated width would have the grid laid out afresh at every frame of it.
- The Recent strip goes back to its start when the list is shown again (a search, a filter, a reload), and Shift with the wheel does nothing over the grid.
- The Library reads its games and the Recent strip again each time it is shown, so an account renamed or deleted on the Accounts screen shows at once on the cards ("removed account" for a deleted one). Its posters stay decoded when the list is the same.
- "No launchers found" comes from the last scan alone. Nothing reports a launcher's status (`launchers:status`), so a start whose scan fails says "No games found".
- The scan a start runs is asked for by the window, after the Library was first read. The Electron app's main process runs it; the result on screen is the same.
- Nothing reloads the Library after an artwork fetch, because nothing fetches artwork yet. A scan and a launch do.
- Not seen in a capture, because each lasts a few milliseconds on fake launchers: "Scanning your launchers…" with its disabled button, "Scanning…" on Rescan, and the turning Play mark of a one-click Play.
- A database that cannot be opened shows its message in place of the grid ("Could not load the library: …") and the app keeps running. The Electron app shows the box "One Launch could not start", with the log and the data folder, and exits with 1.
- A read of the Library that fails after it has shown its games, after a scan or a launch, leaves the games on screen and says "Could not load the library: …" beside Rescan for six seconds, as a failed scan is told. The Electron app puts that sentence in place of the grid until a read succeeds. On purpose: the games on screen are the last ones read, and a passing fault no longer empties the page.
- The Filter and Sort menus take the mouse and Escape only: no arrow keys, Home, End or Enter, and they stay open while the window is resized.
- Names sort by their lower-cased characters. The Electron app sorts them by the rules of the system language; `triage.toml` X-02 asks for the order of the Windows regional settings, through a native call.
- A poster appears at once, without the 200 ms fade, and the Update badge and the More button have no blur behind them.
- A poster is decoded into a box of 240 by 360 px times the display scale, whatever the card's size.
- The search field has no accessible name or role.
- A click on the empty track of the page's scrollbar does not page, and its thumb shows only while the pointer is below the header.

## Launch dialog, banner and recovery dialog (slices 3 to 5)

What is there: a click on a card or on an entry of the Recent strip opens the Play flow (the account list, the check, the confirm stage with the plan behind "Details", preview mode, the running job with its phases and steps, the hand-over panel, every end of a job), the banner stands for the job while the dialog is closed, and a start asks about a switch the last run did not finish. All of it on fake launchers only: see Steam, Epic, Battle.net and Riot below.

- In the app without the feature `fake`, started without `--real-launchers`, a game opens the dialog, which says that the launcher is not available in this build yet: that run reaches no launcher at all. Started with it, the Play flow runs on this PC's launchers; it has not been run in the window that way (see Real ports).
- The Play flow of a Riot game was run in the window on fake launchers, in captures: the start's scan and Rescan show VALORANT, a click on it lists the three accounts, Play as Riot Alt ends in "Launched as Riot Alt" (One Launch pressed Play on the scripted client), and with a client that refuses that request in "Switched to Riot Alt" with the sentence that hands Play over, as `086-launch-done-handoff-riot` shows it. Also seen: the hand-over panel for a saved sign-in the client refuses (`081-launch-handover-riot`, without its "Copy login"), the refusal while a Riot game runs (`096-launch-game-running-riot`), the confirm stage for a client that runs, the details of a finished job with no token in them, and the blocked stage, which a missing session file gives. `ol-ui` was not changed for Riot. Not seen in the window: a sign-in by hand that comes, a game the client wants to update, and the hand-over with "Start Riot games for you" turned off, which waits for the Settings screen.
- The Play flow of an Epic game was not run in the window. `ol-ui` was not changed for Epic: it asks the backend the same questions as for Steam, and the backend's answers are what the replays and the tests of `ol-api` hold. The owner's check is to play Rocket League® as Alt on fake launchers.
- The Play flow of a Battle.net game was run in the window on fake launchers, in captures: Rescan shows Overwatch, a click on it lists the three accounts, Play as Alt ends in "Launched as Alt", and the details name each step with no token in them. `ol-ui` was not changed for Battle.net. Not seen in the window: the confirm stage for a client that runs, the hand-over of an account without a saved token, and a game the client refuses.
- Without a way to reach the launcher of an unfinished switch, nothing is asked at start and the journal stays where it is. The app without the feature `fake` therefore asks only when started with `--real-launchers`.
- The hand-over panel has no "Copy login" and no "Copy password": the copy to the clipboard is not built (`accounts:copy`).
- An empty account list says "No Steam accounts yet." and offers Close. "Import Steam accounts" waits for the import dialog; "Add … account", which opens the form and comes back to the game's Play with the new account picked (`returnTo`), is not built, and neither is the sentence that points to them.
- A switch without a game ("Switch account", from an account's page) has no way in.
- The dialogs take the mouse, Enter and Escape. Enter presses the button drawn with the ring. There is no Tab order, no focus trap, and the keyboard does not go back to what opened the dialog.
- No tooltips: the launcher's game id on the badge, Stop, Hide, an entry of the Recent strip.
- The text of a plan and of a step's detail cannot be selected or copied.
- The plan behind "Details" ends with "This is a preview: nothing was changed." and leaves out "Generated <date and time>". The window now knows the PC's time zone (`ol_platform_win::clock::local_offset`, handed to `ol_ui::init`), which the line can use.
- Preview mode can be turned off from the dialog. The sidebar's "Preview mode is on" card and the setting itself wait for the Settings screen.
- No line for a screen reader says what the job is doing.
- Not seen in a capture, because each lasts a few milliseconds on fake launchers: "Checking Steam…" and "Stopping…".

## Data (slices 2 and 6)

- Neither build has read a vault key the other wrapped. The Rust vault is checked against the recorded vectors in `oracle/takeover/vault/` and against a key of its own under real DPAPI. The owner's `--doctor` run on a copy of real data settles reading; `oracle/takeover/vault/make-local-fixture.mjs` would settle both directions.
- A vault key that does not unwrap is never replaced. The "clear stored secrets" flow has to decide what happens then.
- Two first starts at once on a fresh folder can each write a vault key. The single-instance lock closes this.
- Not done at start: storing the vault probe when it is absent. The vault opens at start with `--real-launchers`, with fake launchers (the stand-in), and otherwise with the data folder's own key under DPAPI (`Backend::start_with_vault_of_data_folder`), which a first run makes; a backend started with `Backend::start`, which the app no longer does, has none and refuses to store a password (`VAULT_UNAVAILABLE`).
- Nothing is written to `logs\` yet. `database copied` and `database copy failed` go to the console; `starting` and `database migrated` are not written at all.
- The check that refuses the real data folder does not look at links inside an allowed folder: `onelaunch.db` as a hard link to the real database, or `chromium` as a junction into the real folder. A mapped network drive and a `subst` drive were not tried.
- That check and its plain-path rule go away in the installed build (slice 6), which runs on the real folder.
- A release build has no console, so `--doctor` there needs its output redirected to a file.
- `Secret::expose()` returns the text, and the fields of `Account`, `Game` and `Settings` are public. Their `Debug` output is redacted, but a caller can still format a field. Look at every such call in the security review.
- `oracle/fixtures/README.md` reads as 2 accounts with passwords; the fixture database holds 3. The tests assert 3. The README is the owner's to correct.

## Steam (slice 3)

What is there: on fake launchers the backend finds Steam, scans its games into the Library, lists and imports the accounts it remembers, plans and runs a switch (snapshot, journal, the writes, rollback, restore, recovery of an interrupted switch) and starts a game. Proven against the oracle: the 21 files of `oracle/l1/steam` and the two path files of `oracle/l1/core` (1,468 of 1,482 cases; the 14 others hold half a surrogate pair, a fraction or a number where the Rust type takes only text or a whole number); eight scripts of `oracle/l2` that turn on Steam alone, and the Steam jobs of `import` and `handover`; five of the Steam adapter's goldens in `oracle/tests`.

- The real ports are in `ol-platform-win` (see Real ports) and reach the app only through `--real-launchers`, in the build without the feature `fake`. Without it `Backend::start` has no launchers: every launcher command answers `NOT_IMPLEMENTED` and a scan finds nothing. No real switch has been run yet; the first is the owner's.
- The app reaches the fakes only when built with the feature `fake` and started with `--fake-launchers=<folder>`. The fake machine's file port reaches that folder and `backups\` of the data folder, and nothing else. The folder is held to the plain-path rule of the data folder, and a scenario is laid down only in an empty or a new folder. Refused are a folder that holds anything but a scenario's own files and folders, and one whose `steam\steam.exe`, `EpicGamesLauncher.exe`, `Battle.net.exe`, `Battle.net Launcher.exe` or `riot\RiotClientServices.exe` is not an empty file. A folder that passes both is taken at its word.
- A fake machine in the app keeps real time (`FakeClock::following_the_system`), so the age of a remembered session is counted from today: from 2026-11-10 on, `user_1` of the fixtures is past 60 days and every plan for it carries the warning about an expired session, which the Electron app's shots of 2026-11-06 do not show.
- The fake machine's registry is built afresh from the scenario at every start of the app, as the Electron app's is: what a switch wrote there, Steam's `AutoLoginUser` and Battle.net's token under `UnifiedAuth`, is gone after a restart, and a Battle.net token the scripted client issued in an earlier run is unknown to it. The files under the fake launchers' folder stay.
- Still without an interface: the account import and the Backups tab.
- The replay compares a recorded run whole (`ol_replay::check`): every platform event, the job events, and of the final state the accounts, the four launchers' games and saved sign-ins (each by the digest of what the vault sealed), the launch history, the settings, the journal, the backups (a Battle.net snapshot's tokens by the digest of what was sealed), `loginusers.vdf`, `GameUserSettings.ini`, `Battle.net.config`, `RiotGamesPrivateSettings.yaml` and every registry value. Where a recording gives the digest of a projection, because the scripted Riot Client stamps the time into the file it writes, the replay's content is projected the same way: the two token times masked. Not compared, because nothing builds them yet: artwork, the vault probe, the log lines and the copies to the clipboard.
- All 22 scripts of `oracle/l2` are replayed whole. `first-run` is replayed with its two reads of `launchers:status` and its read of `artwork:status` passed over: neither command exists, and a read leaves nothing behind.
- A `crash` step of the replay does not end a process: it leaves the switch's thread standing at the wait it is in, for good, and starts the app again beside it. What is on disk is what a crash would leave; the first database connection stays open until the test ends.
- `oracle/tests/goldens`: of the 74 Steam adapter goldens only the five dry-run plans are checked. The 69 others (real switches, preflights, restores, health checks) and the pipeline goldens need the set-up of each Electron test rebuilt.
- Not built of the Steam adapter: `installFromExe` (Locate), the health check and `launchers:status`.
- Not built around it: the rules of `settings:set` (`Backend::set_setting` stores what it is given), the copy to the clipboard (`accounts:copy`), the artwork queue after a scan, the `instant` flag of `launch:start` that lets the window hide, and the prompt when the app quits during a switch (`Backend::shut_down` stops the switch and waits for it).
- Nothing is logged: the Electron app writes a line for each start, refusal, failure and rollback of a switch, with account names masked.
- The launch job runs on a thread of its own and the adapter is plain blocking code over the ports, where `architecture.md` planned one thread and an executor. The owner has not decided whether to keep it. Since Epic, that thread asks the database's thread for what a switch needs of the accounts and their saved sign-ins (`StoreThread`), and waits for the answer.
- Launcher-owned writes are the thirteen functions at the top of `crates/ol-services/src/switch.rs`: Steam's file and its two registry values, Epic's file, Battle.net's file and the set and the removal of a value under its `UnifiedAuth` key, Riot's file, the restore of each launcher, and the one write of a snapshot's files that the restores of Steam, Epic and Battle.net share. Nothing but review keeps another module from asking a writer port for the same.

## Epic (slice 4)

What is there: on fake launchers the backend finds the Epic Games Launcher, scans its games into the Library, lists the accounts that signed in on the PC, says what is saved of an account's sign-in and whose session the launcher holds now (`Backend::session`), plans and runs a switch (the window close, the outgoing session saved, the encrypted snapshot, the write of `GameUserSettings.ini`, the sign-in read from the launcher's log, the hand-over, the new session saved, a wrong account, rollback, restore, recovery) and starts a game. Proven against the oracle: the 13 files of `oracle/l1/epic` (995 of 1,044 cases; of the 49 others 10 hold half a surrogate pair, 7 are a file with CRLF that ends in a bare LF and 8 a Data value of 8 characters or fewer (both below), 18 are refusals of text that is not JSON, worded by the JavaScript engine, 4 are nested deeper than 128 levels, 1 holds a number past the largest float and 1 is a session with its keys in another order than the Rust type's); of `oracle/l2`, `play-epic`, `handover`, `wrong-account`, `quit-during-switch` and `recovery` in full, and the Epic jobs of `play-all` and `unowned`, the restore of a pinned snapshot at the end of `unowned` among them.

- A saved sign-in rests in two places: the column `blob_enc` of `launcher_sessions`, sealed by the vault, and a snapshot's copy of `GameUserSettings.ini`, sealed the same way as `<relative>.enc`. In memory it is wiped after use where it is a value of its own (`Secret`, `EpicSessionBlob`, the JSON around it). The text of the settings file, which holds it once it is written, is not wiped.
- `Backend::seed_session` stores a sign-in as it is given, for the fake launchers' seed and the tests. Only a build with the feature `fake` has it: that of `ol-api`, which turns on that of `ol-services`. The app without the feature saves a sign-in only from the launcher's own file. The tests of `ol-api` need the feature, and `cargo xtask check` turns it on through `ol-replay`.
- Not built: saving the launcher's current session from an account's page (`sessions:capture`), forgetting one (`sessions:forget`), `installFromExe` (Locate), the health check and `launchers:status`.
- The 48 Epic adapter goldens of `oracle/tests/goldens` are not checked.
- The folders `%LOCALAPPDATA%` and `%ProgramData%` come from whoever builds `Launchers` (`EpicMachine`). The fakes put the scenario's in; with `--real-launchers` the app puts in the environment's, read by `ol-platform-win` (`machine::folders`), and keeps the default only for a variable that is not set.
- Not exercised by any test or recorded run: a launcher that starts itself again after it was closed (the three tries of the shutdown), two settings files at once (`Config\Windows` and `Config\WindowsEditor`), a session that changes at every start (`rotateOnStart`), and a sign-in that fails for a reason other than a refused token.
- Carried over from the Electron app, because `oracle/l1/epic` records it, and against "every other byte left alone" on a file no launcher writes: in a settings file that mixes CRLF and bare LF a rewrite makes every line end CRLF, as Steam's rewrite of `loginusers.vdf` does. Keeping each line's own end would differ from the recorded cases on purpose. The owner decides.
- Unlike the Electron app, on purpose: a switch that closed the launcher reads the settings file again even when the plan found no outgoing session to save, and saves one that a sign-in made during the close left for a linked account; the step then shows that save and nothing otherwise. And a sign-in by hand that the switch's first look already finds has its session saved, as one found later does; the Electron app left that step waiting. The session saved after a sign-in is read while the log still names the account that signed in. Once the log names another account (by the rule the sign-in was confirmed by: another Epic id than the account's, or one another One Launch account holds), nothing is saved and the switch ends as a wrong account, as one seen at the sign-in does: the snapshot goes back and no game is started. Once the log names no sign-in, or an id it cannot tell from the account's, only the save is left out ("Not saved: …"). The Electron app saves what the file holds for the account the switch was for and carries on. The log is read after the file, so a launcher that writes a new account's session before it logs that sign-in can still have it saved. No recorded run reaches these cases.

## Battle.net (slice 4)

What is there: on fake launchers the backend finds the Battle.net client (the folder chosen in Settings, the Uninstall key, the command of the `battlenet` link, the default folder), scans its games into the Library (a search of the Uninstall key and one small read for each product, the last play from `Battle.net.config`), lists the logins the client saved, says what is saved of an account's sign-in and whose token the registry holds (`Backend::session`), plans and runs a switch (the window close and then the end of the client alone, the outgoing token saved, the snapshot, the registry writes, the rewrite of `Battle.net.config`, the start through `Battle.net Launcher.exe`, the sign-in read from the client's log, the hand-over, the new token saved, a wrong account, rollback, restore, recovery) and starts a game with `--exec=launch`, watching the log for its start and refusing a game the log says needs an update, is not playable or is running. Proven against the oracle: the 24 files of `oracle/l1/battlenet` (1,130 of 1,377 cases; of the 247 others 211 are refusals of text that is not JSON, worded by the JavaScript engine, 18 hold half a surrogate pair, 11 are values the Rust types do not take, 6 ask the product table for a name every JavaScript object has and 1 is nested deeper than 128 levels); of `oracle/l2`, `play-battlenet`, `game-running`, `import` and `first-run` in full, and the Battle.net jobs of `play-all`, `unowned` and `needs-update`; of `oracle/tests/goldens`, the ten dry-run plans and the two preflights that open six of the Battle.net adapter's goldens.

- A switch writes three things and nothing else: the stored token as a binary value under `HKCU\Software\Blizzard Entertainment\Battle.net\UnifiedAuth`, the removal of every other value under that key, and `%APPDATA%\Battle.net\Battle.net.config` with the login moved to the front of `Client.SavedAccountNames`. The file is rewritten only when reading it and writing it back gives its own bytes; otherwise it is left and the switch ends in the hand-over.
- A saved token rests in two places: the column `blob_enc` of `launcher_sessions`, sealed by the vault, and a snapshot's manifest, where `meta.unifiedAuth` holds each value sealed the same way. A snapshot's copy of `Battle.net.config` is in the clear, as the Electron app's is: it holds the saved logins and no token. In memory a token is wiped after use (`TokenValue`, `Secret`). The test `a_saved_token_is_under_the_clients_key_and_encrypted_in_the_data_folder_and_nowhere_else` of `ol-api` holds a switch, its plan, its job, its errors, the fake machine's log and every file of the data folder to that.
- The plan says the token is written with `reg add /t REG_BINARY`, in the Electron app's words, which the goldens hold. The real registry port writes it with `RegSetValueExW` as `REG_BINARY`, and no `reg.exe` runs. Whether the sentence stays is the owner's.
- Not built: saving the client's current token from an account's page (`sessions:capture`), forgetting one (`sessions:forget`), `installFromExe` (Locate), the health check and `launchers:status`. `parse_aggregate` is held to its recorded cases and nothing calls it: only the health check reads `aggregate.json`.
- Of the 42 Battle.net adapter goldens of `oracle/tests/goldens`, 36 are not checked, and of the six that are, only the plans and preflights they open with: the real switches, restores and launches need the set-up of each Electron test rebuilt.
- The folders `%APPDATA%`, `%LOCALAPPDATA%` and `%ProgramData%` come from whoever builds `Launchers` (`BattlenetMachine`), as Epic's do.
- Unlike the Electron app, on purpose, because the client writes its token at a sign-in and `Battle.net.config` when it exits, after the plan has looked: a switch that closed the client reads the token again even when the plan found no outgoing sign-in to save, and saves one a sign-in during the close left for a linked account; the saved-accounts step reads the file again when the plan found the login first, writes it when the client put another login first meanwhile, and decides from that read whether a refused token is stale. Either step shows only what it did after the close. A sign-in by hand that the first look already finds has its token saved, as the plan's last step says; the Electron app left that step waiting. The token saved after a sign-in is the one value under `UnifiedAuth` that was not there when the switch started the client, read while the log still names the account that signed in: a value that was there before (one another program left beside the account's, which a switch that found the account's token in place does not remove), two new values, or a log that no longer names the sign-in save nothing, and a sign-in as another account meanwhile (by the rule the sign-in was confirmed by) saves nothing and ends the switch as a wrong account, as one seen at the sign-in does: the snapshot goes back and no game is started. The Electron app saves the first value that is not the one the switch wrote, which can be another account's. The log is read after the values, so a client that writes a new account's token before it logs that sign-in, under the same value name, can still have it saved. No recorded run reaches these cases.
- Not exercised by any test or recorded run: a client that starts itself again after it was closed (the three tries of the shutdown), one that ignores the window close and is ended, a token the client rejects (the saved sign-in marked stale), a sign-in as the wrong account, a manual sign-in that never comes, a registry write that fails, the `uri` form of a launch, and a game without a launch code.

## Riot (slice 4)

What is there: on fake launchers the backend finds the Riot Client (the folder chosen in Settings, the client's Uninstall entry, `RiotClientInstalls.json`, the command of the `riotclient` link, the default folder), scans its games into the Library (the metadata folders under `%ProgramData%\Riot Games`, the last play from `RiotClientSettings.yaml`, an update the newest run's log reports), names the one account whose sign-in `RiotGamesPrivateSettings.yaml` holds, says what is saved of an account's sign-in (`Backend::session`), plans and runs a switch (the refusal while a Riot game runs, the window close of the client and then of the window processes it leaves behind, the outgoing sign-in saved, the encrypted snapshot, the write of the file, the start with the game's launch arguments, the sign-in read from the client's log and the account read from the file, the hand-over, the new sign-in saved, a wrong account, cancel, rollback, restore, recovery) and hands a game to the client (the shortcut's two arguments, the watch of the log and of the image names, the press of Play through a port, the hand-over to the user when Play is not pressed or does not take). Proven against the oracle: the 16 files of `oracle/l1/riot` (709 of 715 cases; of the 6 others 4 hold half a surrogate pair and 2 give `launchArgs` a product id that no folder name gives, which the Rust type rules out); of `oracle/l2`, `play-riot`, `riot-stay-signed-in`, `play-all`, `unowned` and `needs-update` in full; of `oracle/tests/goldens`, the plans and preflights of seven of the Riot adapter's goldens, 15 calls, a refused preflight among them.

- Riot's rules (`docs/adapters/riot.md` in the Electron repo) are held by how the code is cut where it can be. The adapter is handed the files and the registry to read only. All it can do to a process goes through `ClientProcesses` (`ol-adapters/src/riot/processes.rs`): the image names that run, a start, and a window close or an end of the client's own two images, the end never with what the image started; there is no way through it to a path or to another image. The Riot switch (`ol-services/src/switch/riot.rs`) reaches no process and no registry port itself. A Riot game is known by `ProcessLister::running_names`, a listing that opens no process, and a listing that fails is an error to the switch, the restore and the launch. The one function that closes the client, `shutdown`, looks for a Riot game directly before every close and every end, the later rounds included, and closes or ends nothing beside one. The real port lists that way, from the kernel's process table, with no `tasklist`, `taskkill`, WMI or `Get-Process`; `ProcessLister::list_processes`, which the Steam, Epic and Battle.net adapters ask for the path of a game of theirs, gives a Riot game, Vanguard and what they started by name alone (see Real ports).
- A switch writes one thing: `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Riot Games\Riot Client\Data\RiotGamesPrivateSettings.yaml`, whole, as the account's saved copy of it, or with `psl.authorization.riot-client` set to `null` and every other byte as it was. Both writes and the restore go through `write_riot_session` in `switch.rs`, which refuses a file that is not there, and which takes, and uses up, a `ReadyToWrite` that only `switch/riot.rs` can make, and only from a look taken directly before that write: one process listing that shows no Riot game and the client still closed (a listing that cannot be read refuses the write), after the client was seen closed and the sign-in the file held was handed to the store for the account it belongs to. The switch, the rollback and the restore each take that look after the snapshot and the store, directly before they write. Nothing is deleted, and nothing in the registry is written.
- As in the Electron app, and so not a promise: a store that fails does not stop the write that follows it. In a switch the step then reads "Not saved" and the snapshot still holds the file; before a rollback there is no other copy, and before a rollback or a restore nothing says so, because nothing logs yet. The last look is one process listing and then one read of the file, which has to be the file the store read, by the SHA-256 of its text: a file that changed since, because the client ran and exited in between, or that is gone or cannot be read (a file that is not UTF-8 text among them, so a restore over one is refused too), refuses the write with nothing written. What remains is the time from that listing to the write: a Riot game or the client that starts in it is not seen, unless the client has changed the file by the time of the read. A read of the file that fails while the client runs, after a sign-in by hand, fails the switch and puts the file back. A line inside the `riot-client` block that is indented with a tab ends the block for the reader. A comment no deeper than `riot-client:` with more of the block below it is refused as a shape the reader does not know, so nothing is saved from such a file and no sign-out is written over it: the Electron app cuts the block at the comment and writes the rest of it back under `riot-client: null`, which the client cannot read. The client writes neither. Each is the owner's to decide once the real ports show what a real client does.
- A saved sign-in rests in two places: the column `blob_enc` of `launcher_sessions`, sealed by the vault, and a snapshot's copy of the file, sealed the same way as `Data/RiotGamesPrivateSettings.yaml.enc`. In memory the file's text is wiped after use wherever the Riot code holds it (`RiotSessionBlob`, `Secret`, the JSON that is sealed, which is written into a buffer that never grows); the plan keeps only a fingerprint. What a snapshot reads of a file before it seals it, and what a restore takes out of the vault, is wiped too, for every launcher. Not wiped: what the file port holds inside itself while it reads and writes, and the client's log, which is read as text, up to 8 MB at a time, and on a real client names accounts. The test `a_sign_in_is_in_the_clients_own_file_and_sealed_in_the_data_folder_and_nowhere_else` of `ol-api` holds a switch, its plan, its job, its errors, the fake machine's log and every file of the data folder to that, for the tokens, the cookies and the Riot ID.
- The launch request to the client's own local server has a port, `RiotLocalLaunch` in `ol-platform-api`, and no real channel: only `FakeRiotLaunch`, which hands the request to the scripted client as a press of Play and reads no lockfile. A machine without a channel (`RiotMachine`'s default, and so the real one until the owner decides) leaves Play to the user. `parse_lockfile` is held to its recorded cases and nothing calls it.
- `Backend::seed_session` takes a Riot sign-in too, for the fake launchers' seed. As for Epic and Battle.net, only a build with the feature `fake` has it.
- Not built: saving the client's current sign-in from an account's page (`sessions:capture`), forgetting one (`sessions:forget`), `installFromExe` (Locate), the health check, `launchers:status` with its running game, and the product's icon as artwork (`gameIcon`). Nothing is logged, so no Riot ID is handed to a log's redaction: the Riot ID is read for the name it suggests for an account nobody holds, and goes nowhere else.

## Real ports (slices 3 and 4)

What is there: `ol-platform-win` has a real registry, process, file, shell and clock port, and `machine::platform()` puts them together. The app attaches them only with `--real-launchers`, and opens the data folder's vault for them (`ol_api::launchers::on_this_pc`). The registry and file ports pass the contract suite the fakes pass (`ol_platform_api::contract`); the process port is tested on copies of `ol-test-child` and, for Riot's games and Vanguard, on a made-up process table behind its `System` seam.

- Not run on a real launcher by anyone: a scan, a close, an end, a start, a link, a write. What the real launchers do with them is the owner's first switch to show.
- Riot gets no channel for the launch request: Play is left to the user, and a Riot launch ends in the hand-over.
- The process table is read with `NtQuerySystemInformation` (`SystemProcessInformation` for the names, ids, parents and start times, `SystemProcessIdInformation` for a file), as the Electron app's own scripts read it; the row layout is checked on every read against this process's own name and start time, and a table that does not agree is an error.
- A kill chooses its targets from the table, reads the table again just before it opens them and drops any whose row is gone, changed or now shielded; a path is asked only of a process the table shows unchanged just before, and dropped when it has changed just after. What is left: a process that ends between that last read and the call, and whose id Windows hands at once to a new process, has that new process opened (by a kill) or its path asked (by a listing). A kill then ends nothing, as the start time of what it opened is not the one chosen, and a listing drops the path. The same holds for a window handle between the listing of the windows and the close. A port that acts by id or by handle cannot close that window.
- A Riot game or Vanguard shields what it started through the parent ids in the table, and a process whose parent has exited is linked to nothing there. So the port remembers, by id and start time, each process it saw shielded, and shields it and whatever it starts for as long as it runs; every listing, close and end reads the table. That holds only for a link the port read while it stood: a helper whose go-between exited before any read of this run is a stranger to the port. Its path is asked by `list_processes` (from the kernel's table of ids and images: it is not opened), and a close or an end that names its own image reaches it; the adapters name only a launcher's own images. No tree end reaches it, as an id taken again belongs to a younger process, which is no parent (a clock set back aside). What is remembered can also be too much: a process shielded because its parent's id was taken by a Riot game stays shielded until it exits, so an end leaves it and the launcher's shutdown then says it is still running.
- A put-back that itself fails leaves the file named in the error with its new content and its old content beside it, as `<file>.onelaunch-<pid>-<count>-<clock>.old`. The old content is kept by a hard link; where the disk cannot make one, by a copy, and a target put back from a copy is a new file with the old bytes, without the old one's attributes or permissions.
- The protected images are `ol_platform_api::protected::PROTECTED_IMAGES`. A Riot game that ships under a new image name is not protected until it is added there; a test of `ol-adapters` holds the list to every game image of `ol-formats`' product table.
- A program started by its 8.3 path runs under a short name such as `VALORA~1.EXE`. A name with `~` and a digit whose first two characters, spaces left out, are those of a protected image is protected too; any other program whose name starts so and runs under a short name is shielded as well, which costs nothing, and none of the launchers' own images does. What counts as a Riot game running is narrower, because a stranger's short name there would refuse every Riot switch: the game image's first six characters, `~` and a number, or its first two, four hex digits, `~` and a number (Vanguard's never count). Whether Windows reports a short name for such a process at all is not verified.

## Differences from the Electron app, on purpose

None of those about stored values and launcher files is visible on data the app wrote itself. The last four are about the window and the fake launchers.

- A stored value shorter than 31 bytes, the empty one included, is `VAULT_UNREADABLE`. So is a decrypted value that is not UTF-8.
- Integer settings parse strictly: `35px` is not 35.
- A non-text value in a text column fails the read and names the column.
- Artwork comes back as the stored relative path, not as an `onelaunch-art://` URL. The Library gets the file's path, joined only when the stored one has the shape `<game id>/<kind>.<ext>`.
- `PRAGMA temp_store = MEMORY` on every open, so rows cannot spill into `%TEMP%`.
- The backup copy just made is never pruned. The Electron app prunes by name alone, so after the clock is set back it deletes the copy it has just taken.
- A launcher's snapshot just taken is never removed by its own clean-up either. The Electron app prunes snapshots by name alone, so after the clock is set back, or when a name freed in the same millisecond is taken again, it can delete the snapshot a switch has just taken, and a rollback then finds none.
- The Library's sort is the row `library.sort` of the settings table (`name` or `lastPlayed`), as `triage.toml` X-01 decides. The Electron app keeps the choice in its page's storage and never reads that row; the choice made there is not taken over.
- A last-played time that is not a full timestamp (`2026-01-02T03:04:05.678Z`) leaves the card's line without a time.
- A blank character outside a string that is none of space, tab, carriage return, line feed and byte order mark (a form feed, a no-break space) is skipped in a VDF file. The Electron app never comes back from such a file.
- A number written `-0` in a Steam file is 0.
- A `Timestamp` in `loginusers.vdf` or a `LastPlayed` in an app manifest outside the years 0 to 9999 reads as no time. The Electron app fails the whole account list or the whole scan on one.
- A game folder a manifest names relative to a library folder that is itself not a full path is taken as `steamapps\common`. The Electron app resolves it against the folder it runs in.
- The games a scan finds are ordered by their lower-cased names, as the Library is.
- A failed switch is not put back while Steam, the Epic Games Launcher or the Battle.net client will not close: nothing is written, the job says why ("Restoring backup <id> also failed: …"), and the snapshot stays for the next start to offer, as for Riot. The Electron app writes the snapshot back under the running launcher and reports it restored.
- A first sign-in for an account Steam has never seen that lands on another account `loginusers.vdf` names fails the switch: "Steam signed in as <that account> instead of <login>.", and no game is started. The Electron app takes any new sign-in for the account while Steam has no entry for it yet. An id Steam has no entry for is still taken, as there.
- A Steam switch's snapshot records AutoLoginUser and RememberPassword as they are when it copies `loginusers.vdf`, after Steam has exited. The Electron app records the values its plan read before Steam was closed.
- The rewrite of `loginusers.vdf` changes the accounts of the top-level `users` block alone. The Electron app also changes a block with an account-shaped key under any other top-level key, which its reader never takes for an account.
- A log tail cut short where a line starts keeps that line. The Electron app drops the first line of every tail it cuts.
- A job stopped before its thread runs starts no game, on the way straight to the game too. The Electron app sends the launch.
- A scan that could not read Steam's `libraryfolders.vdf`, list Riot's metadata folder or read one of Riot's product settings files changes nothing in the Library and says so on that launcher's entry. The Electron app keeps what it could read and marks every other game of that launcher as not installed until a later scan.
- A journal whose `startedAt` is not a full timestamp is unreadable and moved aside. The Electron app takes any text its date parser takes.
- A snapshot manifest whose `bytes` is not a whole number from 0 up is unreadable.
- A job is reported as finished once its launch is in `launch_history` and the account's last use is stored. The Electron app reports it first.
- The settings a switch depends on (`steam.path`, `steam.autoLogin`, `backups.keep`) are read when the job starts. The Electron app reads each at the moment it is used.
- The fake process table records a password argument as `<password>`. The Electron app's fake report, a development file, holds it in the clear.
- A launch dialog opened while a job runs shows that job under its own game's name. The Electron app shows the running job under the name of the game that was clicked.
- A double-click on a card is one click: its second click neither plays a second time nor closes the dialog the first one opened.
- A fake run writes no `fake-platform.json` into the data folder. The Electron app's report of what its fake platform was asked to do is a development file.
- The fake launchers are named by the arguments `--fake-launchers`, `--fake-scenario` and `--fake-fixtures`, and the app itself lays the scenario down. The Electron app reads `ONELAUNCH_FAKE_LAUNCHERS` and leaves the laying down to its driver script.
